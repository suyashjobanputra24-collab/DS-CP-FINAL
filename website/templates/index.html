📌 The Central Problem: The Illusion of Point-Estimate Precision

Food-delivery platforms (such as Zomato, Swiggy, Uber Eats, and Meituan) present a single deterministic Estimated Time of Arrival (ETA) to consumers immediately upon order placement: "Your food will arrive in 35 minutes". However, delivery fulfillment is governed by stochastic multi-stage physical events: kitchen preparation queues, courier dispatch latency, and road congestion.

Scenario A: Predictable Order (35 min ETA)

Clear weather, low traffic, rapid kitchen handover. The order arrives in 36 minutes. Negligible customer friction.

Scenario B: High-Uncertainty Order (35 min ETA)

Sudden storm, heavy road gridlock, courier assigned 2 concurrent orders. The order arrives in 54 minutes (19 min late). Results in support tickets, refund requests, and customer churn.

PromiseGuard provides an end-to-end framework to: (1) Predict expected transit time accurately, (2) Quantify the plausible outcome range via 90% prediction intervals, (3) Model the probability of breaching customer SLA promises, (4) Explain delay contributors via SHAP, and (5) Simulate proactive intervention strategies before promises are broken.
